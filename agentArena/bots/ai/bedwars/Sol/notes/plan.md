# Sol's five-team plan

The score rewards surviving whole teams. Four geared players together on the middle are worth more than repeated unsupported bed attacks. Keep the bed as respawn insurance early, build a useful economy, and occupy the final fighting ground before beds vanish.

## Roles and opening

Use stable name order: one defender, one diamond collector, and two paired fighters. All four initially split the forge legally (nearby teammates each receive its iron and gold). With timed digging, the defender saves its first 24 iron for one end-stone pack: eight inner faces and four roof cells. Add an outer wool shell, with at most twelve seconds spent on that optional layer, then return to the forge. Descend off the roof immediately when the forge is lower; do not spend the last blocks bridging at roof height. The defence supplies respawn insurance until the forced end of beds at 60%.

The collector opens the nearest diamond route after stocking a sword, permanent armour, and enough blocks for a return. Prioritise Protection I, Sharpness, and more protection: these benefit the whole central squad. Collect another generator instead of camping an empty one when a stocked one is reachable. Deposit diamonds through actual trips to our upgrades keeper.

The fighters and defender leave together around 2:15 with swords, permanent iron armour, and a generous wool reserve (scaled down if the match is shorter). This replaces the initial split after the first compact practice lost its isolated players to combat. On large maps build a shared way to the middle, using useful stepping stones, and leave space between players on narrow bridges. Do not charge four defenders with two players. Attack an exposed nearby bed only with our pair present, at most one defender, and time to finish before evacuation. Retain a chosen raid until its deadline rather than turning back halfway. Finish a bed already in reach. Respond to attacks at home only when close enough to help; do not reverse a whole-map crossing for an unreachable bed.

## Economy and fighting

Shop only at our keepers. Fighters buy wool and a stone sword first, permanent chain armour, an iron sword, then permanent iron armour and healing. Save their gold for iron armour before buying apples. The defender instead wears chain armour and saves fourteen gold for a bow plus its first arrows, with a bounded extra stop at the forge before joining the squad; iron armour is a later purchase. Rushers carry permanent pickaxes; the primary miner saves for shears before an optional iron-pickaxe upgrade. Select the fastest owned tool using the game's Java break-time function. Continue one covering block until it breaks, then the exposed bed. Decline a distant bed whose cover will take over twelve seconds with the available tools. Preserve enough blocks for travel before luxury gear. Diamonds become team combat upgrades. Apples are eaten before contact, with space for the eating delay.

Fight on wide ground. Two pairs share target ids, using each player's own delayed observations. They select separate nearby enemies when available: four players all swinging at one victim waste damage in Java's half-second hurt window. Allies leave at least 520 ms between useful swings on the same victim, while every individual weapon still obeys its normal recharge. In the final phase, hold the central ground and engage enemies within roughly 4.75 blocks of the middle; do not fan out toward several fights at the island rim. Exploit other teams fighting each other. Regear after every respawn; equipment and blocks lost at death cannot be assumed present.

## Routes on unseen layouts

Read every coordinate, height, and radius from the current landmarks, never from seed-specific paths or team colour. Compact has diamonds on the middle, so a shared central route serves both economy and combat. Standard and flanks offer diamond side routes. Highmid requires ascending before the middle's rim, not walking under it. Archipelago's inner stones reduce void and offer safe staging ground; outer stones are only useful early, since they will be consumed by the border. Estimate crossing blocks with diagonal corner placements and climb costs, not just straight-line distance.

## Bed Destruction and the closing zone

Use the actual match time limit, including the shorter practice limit. Stop new outer raids well before 60%. The defender leaves the bed and the collector stops returning home; all surviving players take their final supplies and rally on the middle's top surface. Begin this transition around 45-50% of the limit, earlier when far away. The team must already have a bridge and spare blocks. Never depend on a future return to a shop after the zone has begun closing.

Once closing starts, movement inward outranks a distant fight, a generator, a bed, and old role assignments. Treat exposure as an emergency even if a nearby enemy is tempting. The final void floor is one block below the island tops: stand on the actual middle surface, clear of any island roof. Maintain a margin that anticipates the moving border, and remain in the eventual circle (roughly radius 9), rather than waiting just inside today's border. Do not chase a player back outside. Ten seconds of accumulated exposure is final, with only half-speed recovery; recovering inside is mandatory after any exposure.
